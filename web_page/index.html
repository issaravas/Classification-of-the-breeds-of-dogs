Tips to Get the Best Out of BreedScope

Improve accuracy by following these simple steps

Use Good Lighting

Make sure your dog is well-lit to avoid shadows that hide its features. Daylight works best.

Frontal Pose

Take the photo from the front, so the dog is directly facing the camera. It helps the AI.

Single Dog Only

Make sure there's only one dog in the image to avoid confusion during prediction.

Avoid Obstructions

Remove accessories that might block facial features, like scarves or hats.

Frequently asked questions

Our system uses a deep learning model trained on thousands of labeled dog images. When you upload a photo, the image is preprocessed and passed through a neural network (based on the ResNet18 architecture). The model analyzes key features like ear shape, snout, fur texture, and coloration to predict the breed with high confidence. The predicted breed is then displayed instantly for your reference.

Currently, the tool is optimized to analyze one dog per image. When multiple dogs are present in a single photo, the model may struggle to determine which dog to focus on, which can reduce the accuracy of the prediction or result in a misclassification.

To ensure reliable results, we recommend uploading photos where only one dog is clearly visible and centered in the frame. If you want to analyze multiple dogs, it's best to upload separate photos for each one.

Future updates may introduce the ability to detect and analyze multiple dogs in the same image, but for now, focusing on one dog per photo provides the most accurate breed identification.

Our model achieves high accuracy on most popular breeds, especially when the input image is clear. However, results may vary for mixed breeds or unusual angles. We're continuously improving the model with more training data.

Yes, the tool can be used to identify the breed of a puppy, but please note that the accuracy may vary depending on the age and appearance of the puppy.

Puppies often haven’t fully developed the physical traits that are typical of their breed — such as body proportions, fur texture, and facial structure — which are more distinct in adult dogs. As a result, the model may struggle to identify the correct breed with full confidence.

For best results, try to upload clear and well-lit photos where the puppy is facing the camera and is not obstructed by objects or other pets. Keep in mind that as your puppy grows, the predictions will likely become more accurate.
